Leak Detection in Temecula, CA

A rising water bill or a damp spot with no clear source usually means a hidden leak. We trace it electronically anywhere outside the slab in Temecula, CA. Call (951) 467-1450.

Leak detection finds where water is escaping when there's no obvious source, a yard or meter-line leak, a leak inside a wall or ceiling, a failing shutoff valve, or a service line leak between the water meter and the house. This is the page for a leak that isn't visibly under the concrete slab, a leak confirmed or suspected to be under the foundation itself has its own dedicated approach on the slab leak detection page.

What causes this in Temecula

Temecula homes get water through a meter installed and maintained by Rancho California Water District, and everything past that meter belongs to the homeowner, which is where nearly every leak detection call actually originates. The service line running from the meter to the house is the part most likely to fail first: on original construction dating to before the mid-2000s, that run is usually galvanized pipe or copper, and both corrode and eventually pinhole with enough years in the ground. A PEX service line, standard on construction from the mid-2000s onward, resists that kind of corrosion, so a leak on a newer property more often traces back to physical damage, a settling foundation edge, a nail, or a fitting that was never seated quite right, rather than age on its own.

Signs to watch for

A water bill that's climbed with no clear reason is the most common first sign, and it's worth a simple check before calling anyone: shut off every fixture and appliance in the house, then watch the water meter's leak indicator (a small triangular or star-shaped dial on most modern meters) for a minute or two. If it's still moving with everything off, water is escaping somewhere between the meter and the house. Beyond the meter test, a damp patch on a wall or ceiling with no obvious plumbing above it, an unusually soggy or greener-than-normal patch of lawn along the service line's path, or a hissing sound near a wall are all worth investigating.

How it's diagnosed

The meter test comes first, since it confirms whether a real leak exists at all before any equipment is deployed. From there, acoustic listening equipment traces the sound of water escaping under pressure, and tracer gas gets used on a harder-to-isolate section when acoustic alone doesn't narrow it down enough. The goal is a specific, marked location, not a general area, before any digging or wall access happens.

What actually happens on the call

1. Run the meter testEvery fixture and appliance off, then watch the meter's leak indicator to confirm whether water is actually moving through the line.
2. Narrow house-side vs. utility-sideThe meter's position relative to the property line and the test's timing help determine which side of the meter the leak is actually on.
3. Acoustic and tracer-gas tracingEquipment narrows the leak to a specific, marked location before any digging or wall access is discussed.
4. Repair and re-testAfter the fix, the meter test gets run again to confirm the leak is actually resolved, not just assumed fixed.

Repair options

A pinhole leak in an accessible section of galvanized or copper service line is typically a direct spot repair. A line that's corroded in more than one place, common on an original galvanized run that's decades old, is often a better candidate for replacing the full run from meter to house rather than chasing each new pinhole as it appears over the following months. A leak traced to a failing shutoff or fixture valve rather than the line itself is usually a straightforward valve replacement.

Repair or replace?

An original galvanized service line that's already produced one confirmed leak is a reasonable candidate for a full replacement rather than a series of spot repairs, since galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out along its whole length, not just at the point that happened to fail first.

FAQ

How do I check for a hidden leak myself before calling anyone?

Turn off every fixture and appliance in the house, then check the water meter's small leak indicator dial for a minute or two. If it's still spinning or moving with everything off, water is escaping somewhere in the line between the meter and the house.

What if the leak turns out to be on the water company's side of the meter?

Everything before the meter belongs to and is maintained by the utility, not the homeowner, so a confirmed utility-side leak gets reported to Rancho California Water District directly rather than repaired privately.

How is this different from slab leak detection?

This page covers leaks in the yard, walls, ceilings, and the service line between the meter and the house. A leak confirmed or suspected to be under the concrete foundation itself is handled as a slab leak, which uses the same electronic tracing but a different repair approach.
