Toilet Repair & Installation in Temecula, CA

A running toilet, a rocking base, or a clog that won't clear all trace back to specific worn parts. We repair and install toilets in Temecula, CA. Call (951) 467-1450.

Toilet repair and installation covers a running toilet, a clog that a plunger won't clear, a toilet that rocks or leaks at its base, and full replacement for a remodel or an older, water-inefficient model. Most toilet problems trace back to one specific worn part rather than the whole fixture.

What causes this in Temecula

A toilet that's original to an older Temecula home, common in parts of Old Town and established communities like Vail Ranch, is old enough that its wax ring seal and internal fill components are well past a reasonable service life, since both wear out over years regardless of water quality. Federal law has required toilets sold since 1994 to use no more than 1.6 gallons per flush, so a home with its original pre-1994 toilet is both more likely to need parts that are harder to source and a candidate for real water savings from replacement, independent of whether anything has actually failed yet.

Signs to watch for

A toilet that runs intermittently or continuously after flushing almost always means a worn flapper that isn't sealing the tank properly, or a fill valve that isn't shutting off at the right water level. A toilet that rocks slightly when someone sits down, or that has a damp spot or soft flooring around its base, points to a failed wax ring seal, which is worth addressing quickly since that moisture sits directly on the subfloor. A toilet that clogs repeatedly despite normal use, rather than from an obvious cause, is worth checking for a partial blockage further down the line rather than just plunging it again each time.

How it's diagnosed

A running toilet gets diagnosed by checking the flapper's seal and the fill valve's shutoff level directly, since either one produces a similar symptom on its own. A rocking or leaking base gets confirmed by removing the toilet and inspecting the wax ring and the flange underneath it, which also reveals whether the subfloor has taken on any moisture damage. A recurring clog gets checked with a cable or camera when it isn't resolving with normal plunging, since it may be a partial blockage in the branch line rather than the toilet itself.

Repair options

A running toilet is typically a flapper or fill valve replacement, both inexpensive parts that resolve the issue directly. A rocking or leaking base means pulling the toilet, replacing the wax ring, and reseating it properly, with a subfloor check if there's been standing moisture for any length of time. Full replacement makes sense for an older pre-1994 toilet with more than one part wearing out at once, or simply as an upgrade during a remodel, and a new installation gets set and leveled properly rather than just bolted down.

Jiggling the handle or ignoring a slow leak vs. Diagnosed part replacement

Jiggling the handle or ignoring a slow leak

  • A running toilet from a worn flapper wastes real water continuously
  • A rocking base means the wax ring seal is likely already compromised
  • Water at the base can damage subfloor before it's visibly obvious
  • A temporary fix rarely holds once the part has actually failed

Diagnosed part replacement

  • Flapper, fill valve, or flush valve replaced based on what's actually worn
  • Wax ring reseated properly if the toilet is rocking or leaking at the base
  • Subfloor checked for damage on any confirmed base leak
  • New installation sized and set level from the start

What affects the price

Repair vs. replaceAn older toilet needing more than one internal part repaired is often a better value replaced outright, especially given the water savings on a pre-1994 model.
Wax ring vs. internal partsA base leak (wax ring) involves pulling the toilet. A running toilet (flapper or fill valve) usually doesn't.
Flooring conditionA base leak that's been present a while sometimes means the subfloor needs attention alongside the toilet itself.

Repair or replace?

An older toilet that's already needed more than one internal repair, especially a pre-1994 higher-flow model, usually makes more sense to replace outright than to keep repairing part by part, since the water savings alone offset a meaningful share of the replacement cost over time.

FAQ

Why does my toilet keep running after I flush?

It's almost always a worn flapper that isn't sealing the tank fully, or a fill valve that isn't shutting off at the correct water level, and replacing whichever part has failed usually resolves it directly.

Is a rocking toilet urgent?

It's worth addressing promptly rather than waiting, since a rocking toilet usually means the wax ring seal underneath has already failed, and continued use lets water seep onto the subfloor, which is a bigger repair the longer it sits.

Is it worth replacing an old toilet that still works?

If it's an original pre-1994 model, it's using well over twice the water of a current standard toilet on every flush, so replacement can make sense on water savings alone, separate from whether anything has actually broken yet.
